A HOME SYSTEM STARTS WITH YOUR HOME

Make rooftop solar part of your energy plan.

A residential solar system is planned around how your household uses electricity and what your property can support. Roof area, shading, orientation, electrical setup and local utility requirements all matter.

MOS Solar can discuss these factors with you, explain possible system configurations and identify what needs to be checked before a detailed design or quotation is prepared.

FROM SUNLIGHT TO HOME USE

How residential solar works

The arrangement depends on your system type, utility connection and whether energy storage is included.

01

Sunlight reaches the modules

Photovoltaic modules produce direct-current electricity when exposed to sunlight.

02

Power is converted

An inverter converts generated electricity into alternating current for compatible home loads.

03

Your home uses the energy

Solar generation can serve household loads while available; the grid or a battery may be part of the design.

04

Manage imports and storage

Connection, export and backup behavior depend on configuration and applicable utility rules.

Grid-connected solar systems generally shut down during a utility outage unless suitable backup equipment and an approved operating arrangement are included. Confirm outage behavior during system design.

IS HOME SOLAR RIGHT FOR YOU?

Suitable applications and site checks.

Residential rooftop solar may suit detached homes, villas and other eligible residential properties. Site suitability cannot be decided from property type alone.

A design discussion should account for roof condition, usable area, shading, access, electrical connection, household load pattern, sanctioned load and utility requirements.

CONFIGURATION OPTIONS

Choose the system approach that fits.

Each option has different grid and storage behavior. Confirm design details before selecting equipment.

01

On-grid

Works with a utility connection. Backup during outages is not normally available from a standard grid-tied inverter alone.

Explore on-grid systems
02

Off-grid

Designed to operate without a utility connection and typically relies on batteries. Load planning and storage sizing are central.

Explore off-grid systems
03

Hybrid

Combines a grid connection with storage-capable equipment where the design and utility arrangement support it.

Explore hybrid systems
A STARTING POINT, NOT A SYSTEM PROMISE

How home system capacity is considered.

There is no single capacity that suits every household. Monthly and daytime electricity use help frame the discussion, while roof area, shading, orientation, electrical limits, budget and utility rules influence the final design.

01Review recent bills and monthly units
02Check usable roof area and shading
03Discuss daytime loads and backup needs
04Confirm capacity through design and utility checks
