Start with the basics: how solar fits your property

A sample editorial guide to the early questions behind rooftop solar.

Begin with the way you use electricity

Electricity bills can help you understand how much power a property draws from the grid over time. Look across several months and note seasonal changes, occupancy patterns and equipment that runs during daylight hours.

Monthly units are a helpful starting point, but a system design also considers when electricity is used. Solar output changes through the day and across seasons, so consumption timing matters alongside the total bill.

Consider the roof and the site

A site review can consider usable roof area, orientation, shading, access, structural condition and the route for electrical connections. These factors influence the system layout and the practical capacity that may fit.

Photos and a rough roof measurement can support an initial discussion, but they cannot replace an on-site assessment and the technical checks required for a proposal.

Explore the system configuration

Grid-connected, standalone and hybrid systems serve different priorities. Grid-connected systems work with a utility connection. Standalone systems are designed to operate independently and typically include storage. Hybrid systems combine generation, grid connection and storage according to the project design.

Ask how the proposed configuration behaves during an outage, what backup loads it can support, and how energy exports or imports are handled under the applicable utility arrangements.

Ask for clear assumptions

When reviewing a design, request the proposed capacity, expected generation assumptions, equipment schedule, installation scope, applicable approvals and service terms in writing. Savings estimates should explain self-consumption, export credit assumptions and any excluded charges.

Government subsidy eligibility and application requirements can change. Confirm current details through the official scheme portal and your distribution company before making a purchase decision.
